Rabies vaccination is a core public health vaccine for cats. The exact schedule depends on where you live, your cat's history, and the vaccine product used by your veterinarian.

## Typical rabies shot schedule

Most cats follow this pattern:

1. **Initial vaccination** at the age required by local regulations and product labeling.
2. **First booster** one year later.
3. **Ongoing boosters** every 1 or 3 years, depending on the approved product and law.

Your veterinarian should always align recommendations with local legal requirements.

## Why this vaccine matters

Rabies is almost always fatal once clinical signs appear, and it can infect humans. Vaccinating cats helps protect:

- Your cat
- Your family
- Veterinary teams and animal handlers
- Community public health

## How cats get rabies

Cats are typically infected through saliva exposure from a bite wound. Wildlife is the most common source in North America, especially bats and raccoons.

Indoor cats are not risk-free because:

- Bats can enter homes
- Doors/windows can be left open
- Cats can slip outdoors unexpectedly

## Do cats carry rabies?

Infected cats can transmit rabies to other animals and people through bites. This is why prompt post-exposure handling and vaccination records are so important.

<WarningBox title="If your cat is bitten by wildlife">
Contact your veterinarian immediately, even if your cat appears normal. Rabies exposure protocols are time-sensitive and differ by vaccination status.
</WarningBox>

## Side effects and safety

Most cats tolerate rabies vaccines well. Mild temporary effects may include:

- Sleepiness
- Brief soreness at injection site
- Slightly reduced appetite for a day

Severe reactions are uncommon but should be treated as urgent.

## Keeping records current

- Keep paper and digital copies of rabies certificates
- Confirm due dates at every annual exam
- Ask whether your cat is due for a 1-year or 3-year booster
- Verify legal requirements before travel or boarding

If you are unsure whether your cat is overdue, book a vaccine review visit and bring all prior records.
