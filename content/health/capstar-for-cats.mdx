Capstar (nitenpyram) is a fast-acting oral flea medication that kills adult fleas quickly. It is generally safe for many cats when used at the correct dose, but it is not a complete long-term flea plan by itself.

## What Capstar does well

Capstar is useful when you need rapid knockdown of adult fleas, such as:

- New rescue cats with visible flea burden
- Multi-pet homes with an acute outbreak
- Pre-grooming or pre-boarding flea control

It can improve comfort quickly, especially in itchy cats.

## Limits of Capstar

Capstar does **not**:

- Kill flea eggs in the environment
- Provide extended monthly protection
- Fully manage severe household infestations alone

For lasting control, most cats need a veterinarian-recommended long-acting preventive and environmental cleaning.

## Is Capstar safe for every cat?

Safety depends on:

- Body weight and age eligibility
- Concurrent illnesses
- Pregnancy/lactation status
- Other medications and preventives

Always confirm dosing with your veterinarian, especially for kittens, seniors, or medically complex cats.

## Possible side effects

Most effects are mild and short-lived:

- Temporary itching or agitation
- Increased grooming
- Brief vocalization

These can occur as fleas become active before dying. Persistent vomiting, severe lethargy, or neurologic signs warrant immediate veterinary evaluation.

<VetTip title="Use Capstar as part of a full plan">
Think of Capstar as a rapid-response tool, not full prevention. Pair it with monthly flea control and environmental treatment.
</VetTip>

## Building a complete flea protocol

1. Fast knockdown (Capstar) when indicated
2. Ongoing prevention for all pets in the home
3. Wash bedding and vacuum frequently during outbreaks
4. Recheck if itching continues despite treatment

If your cat has flea allergy dermatitis, strict year-round control is essential to prevent recurrent skin inflammation.
