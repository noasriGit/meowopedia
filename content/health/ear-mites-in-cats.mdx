Cats most often get ear mites from direct contact with another infected animal. The parasite spreads easily in close-contact settings, especially homes with multiple pets, shelters, and rescue environments.

## How cats get ear mites

Ear mites are highly contagious. Common transmission routes include:

- Nose-to-nose contact with infected cats
- Shared bedding and blankets
- Shared grooming tools
- Contact with infected dogs or ferrets in the home

Kittens are at highest risk because their immune systems are still developing and they often live in close groups before adoption.

## Can indoor cats get ear mites?

Yes. Indoor cats usually pick up mites after:

- A newly adopted pet enters the household
- A foster animal stays temporarily
- An untreated pet returns from boarding or grooming

Indoor-only status lowers exposure to many parasites, but ear mites can still spread rapidly once they enter a household.

## Signs to watch for

The classic signs are:

- Frequent ear scratching
- Head shaking
- Thick black-brown ear debris (coffee-ground appearance)
- Ear irritation, redness, or odor

Some cats become restless or painful around the head and ears.

## How to get rid of ear mites

Effective treatment usually includes three steps:

1. **Confirm diagnosis** with otoscopic exam or microscopic ear swab.
2. **Use prescription treatment** (topical or systemic) for the full recommended duration.
3. **Treat all in-contact pets** to prevent reinfestation.

<HealthAlertBox title="Do not use random over-the-counter drops">
Some products only reduce wax and debris but do not eliminate mites. Incomplete treatment is a common reason for recurrence.
</HealthAlertBox>

## Preventing reinfestation

- Finish all doses exactly as directed
- Recheck ears if scratching continues
- Wash bedding on hot cycles
- Clean brushes, carriers, and favorite resting surfaces
- Keep monthly parasite prevention current when your veterinarian recommends it

## When to see a vet urgently

Seek prompt care if your cat has:

- Severe pain when ears are touched
- Bleeding or pus-like discharge
- Persistent head tilt
- Loss of balance

Early treatment usually resolves infestations quickly and prevents painful secondary infections.
