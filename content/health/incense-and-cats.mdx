Incense can be a problem for cats, especially those with sensitive airways. The smoke itself is an irritant, and added fragrance compounds may worsen respiratory signs in some cats.

## Why incense can bother cats

Cats have small, sensitive airways and spend much of their time near floors where smoke particles settle. Incense can introduce:

- Particulate matter
- Volatile fragrance chemicals
- Soot and combustion byproducts

These can irritate the nose, throat, and lungs.

## Cats at higher risk

The most vulnerable groups include:

- Cats with asthma
- Cats with chronic bronchitis
- Seniors with reduced respiratory reserve
- Brachycephalic cats with baseline breathing challenges

Even healthy cats may show mild irritation with regular exposure.

## Signs of incense sensitivity in cats

- Sneezing
- Coughing or wheezing
- Faster breathing
- Eye watering
- Restlessness or hiding

<WarningBox title="Breathing changes are urgent">
Open-mouth breathing, bluish gums, or persistent wheezing require immediate emergency veterinary care.
</WarningBox>

## Is occasional incense use okay?

Risk depends on ventilation, product type, and your cat's health status. For many households, the safest option is avoiding incense indoors and using non-combustion alternatives.

If used at all:

- Keep it far from your cat's resting areas
- Maximize ventilation
- Never use in small enclosed rooms
- Stop immediately if any symptoms appear

## Safer alternatives

- Unscented air filtration
- Increased ventilation and humidity control
- Fragrance-free cleaning routines
- Cat-safe environmental enrichment instead of scented products

If your cat already has cough or wheeze, discuss home air triggers with your veterinarian and make a formal asthma action plan.
