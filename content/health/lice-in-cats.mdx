Cats do not get human head lice. Lice are usually species-specific, so the lice that infest people are different from the ones that affect cats.

## Can humans give lice to cats?

In normal circumstances, no. Human lice are adapted to human hair and scalp conditions, while feline lice are adapted to cat fur and skin.

That means:

- Your child with head lice is unlikely to infect your cat
- A cat with feline lice is unlikely to infest people

## How cats actually get lice

Cats usually get lice through close contact with another infested cat or contaminated grooming items. Risk is higher in:

- Shelters with crowding pressure
- Outdoor colonies
- Cats with poor grooming due to illness, obesity, or age

## Signs of lice in cats

- Itching and overgrooming
- Dull, rough, or unkempt coat
- Flaky skin
- Small moving insects or tiny eggs attached to hairs

Lice can look similar to other skin conditions, so confirmation matters.

## Diagnosis and treatment

Veterinarians diagnose lice by coat exam and microscopy. Treatment generally includes:

1. Cat-safe topical or systemic parasiticide
2. Repeat treatment per life-cycle timing
3. Cleaning of bedding, brushes, and resting areas
4. Treating all exposed cats when advised

<HealthAlertBox title="Avoid dog-only parasite products">
Some products safe for dogs are toxic to cats. Use only cat-labeled medications prescribed or approved by your veterinarian.
</HealthAlertBox>

## Prevention

- Maintain routine parasite prevention
- Isolate and check new cats before full introduction
- Groom longhaired cats regularly
- Address medical conditions that reduce self-grooming

If your cat has severe itching or skin wounds, schedule prompt care to prevent secondary bacterial infection.
