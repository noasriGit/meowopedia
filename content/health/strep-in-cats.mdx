Cats can get streptococcal infections, but they do not commonly present exactly like human strep throat. In cats with throat discomfort, veterinarians often evaluate for viral respiratory disease, oral pain, inflammation, and secondary bacterial infection.

## Is "strep throat" the right term for cats?

In human medicine, strep throat usually refers to *Streptococcus pyogenes* pharyngitis. In cats, throat signs are more often part of a broader upper respiratory syndrome, with possible bacterial involvement but different organisms and patterns.

## Signs that look like throat infection

- Reduced appetite or painful swallowing
- Hoarse or quiet vocalization
- Sneezing and nasal discharge
- Fever
- Lethargy
- Drooling in some cases

These signs are not specific to strep and need veterinary evaluation.

## Can cats catch strep from people?

Transmission from humans to cats appears uncommon. Still, good hygiene is sensible:

- Wash hands before handling food and bowls
- Avoid face-to-face contact when either species is sick
- Clean shared surfaces regularly

## Diagnosis in cats

Veterinarians may use:

- Physical exam and oral/throat assessment
- Respiratory PCR panels
- Cytology or bacterial culture in selected cases
- Imaging if deeper airway disease is suspected

<VetTip title="Do not self-start antibiotics">
Unnecessary antibiotics can delay diagnosis and contribute to resistance. Testing helps choose the right treatment.
</VetTip>

## Treatment and recovery

Treatment is cause-based and may include:

- Supportive hydration and nutrition
- Anti-inflammatory or pain support
- Targeted antibiotics when bacterial infection is documented
- Isolation from other cats during contagious illness

Most mild upper respiratory cases improve with supportive care, but persistent fever, breathing changes, or refusal to eat requires urgent follow-up.
