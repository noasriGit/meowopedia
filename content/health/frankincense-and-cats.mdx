Frankincense is often marketed as a natural wellness product, but natural does not always mean safe for cats. Concentrated oils are the biggest concern, especially in diffusers or topical use.

## Why frankincense can be risky for cats

Cats have unique liver metabolism and are sensitive to many aromatic compounds. Risk increases with:

- High-concentration essential oils
- Poorly ventilated rooms
- Direct skin exposure
- Repeated exposure over time

Cats also groom frequently, so residues on fur or paws can be ingested.

## Diffusers and airborne exposure

Diffusers may aerosolize compounds that irritate feline airways. This matters most for cats with:

- Asthma
- Chronic cough
- Prior respiratory disease

Signs of intolerance may include sneezing, cough, rapid breathing, watery eyes, or behavior changes.

## Ingestion and skin contact

Accidental licking or topical contact can cause:

- Drooling
- Vomiting
- Lethargy
- Tremors in severe cases

<HealthAlertBox title="Do not apply essential oils to cats">
Topical essential oils can be absorbed through skin and then ingested during grooming. Avoid direct use unless your veterinarian has specifically prescribed a product.
</HealthAlertBox>

## What to do after exposure

1. Remove your cat from exposure area.
2. Stop diffuser/incense use immediately.
3. If oil touched fur, call your veterinarian before bathing; inappropriate products can worsen absorption.
4. Seek urgent care for vomiting, tremors, or breathing changes.

## Safer household approach

- Keep essential oils out of cat-access areas
- Use fragrance-free products when possible
- Improve ventilation
- Use veterinarian-approved environmental strategies for stress support

If you are unsure whether a frankincense product is safe, assume caution and verify with your veterinary team before use.
