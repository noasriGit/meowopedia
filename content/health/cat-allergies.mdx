Cats are most commonly allergic to flea saliva, certain food proteins, and environmental allergens. Most allergic cats show skin signs first, not sneezing.

## Common allergy triggers in cats

### 1) Flea allergy dermatitis

Some cats react intensely to flea saliva. Even a small number of bites can cause severe itching and skin lesions.

### 2) Food allergy

Food allergy usually involves a protein source and can cause:

- Year-round itch
- Face and neck scratching
- Ear inflammation
- Gastrointestinal signs in some cats

### 3) Environmental allergy (atopy)

Triggers can include dust mites, molds, and pollens. Signs may be seasonal or year-round.

## What allergy symptoms look like

- Overgrooming (especially belly, legs, and flanks)
- Hair thinning or bald patches
- Scabs on head/neck ("miliary dermatitis")
- Ear irritation
- Recurrent skin infections

Cats often lick instead of visibly scratching, so overgrooming is a major clue.

## How veterinarians diagnose allergies

There is no single test that diagnoses all cat allergies. Diagnosis typically uses stepwise exclusion:

1. Strict flea prevention trial
2. Elimination diet trial (8-12 weeks)
3. Assessment for secondary infections
4. Additional testing for environmental components as needed

<VetTip title="Diet trials must be strict">
Even small unapproved treats can invalidate a food allergy trial. Consistency is essential for accurate diagnosis.
</VetTip>

## Treatment options

Most cats need a long-term plan that may include:

- Year-round flea prevention
- Prescription diet if food allergy confirmed
- Anti-itch and anti-inflammatory medications
- Management of bacterial or yeast secondary infections
- Environmental control for dust and irritants

## Home management tips

- Keep flea control current for every pet in the house
- Track flare timing, foods, and exposure changes
- Use fragrance-free cleaners and litter when possible
- Follow recheck timelines to adjust treatment

With consistent management, many allergic cats can stay comfortable and maintain healthy skin.
