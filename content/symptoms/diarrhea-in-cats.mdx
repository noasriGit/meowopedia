Diarrhea describes stool that is softer, more watery, or more frequent than your cat's normal pattern. One isolated loose stool is different from repeated diarrhea that causes dehydration or weight loss.

## Common Causes of Diarrhea in Cats

| Cause | Typical pattern |
|------|---|
| Sudden diet change | Starts soon after new food or treats |
| Food intolerance | Recurrent loose stool with certain foods |
| Parasites | Intermittent diarrhea, mucus, poor weight gain |
| Infection | Acute diarrhea, sometimes with fever |
| Inflammatory bowel disease | Chronic or waxing/waning signs |
| Chronic disease | May include appetite, weight, or hydration changes |

## Normal vs Emergency

A short mild episode can be monitored if your cat remains bright, hydrated, and eating.

<HealthAlertBox title="Get urgent care now for severe signs">
Seek immediate care for repeated watery stool with vomiting, blood or black stool, pronounced lethargy, painful abdomen, or signs of dehydration such as tacky gums and weakness.
</HealthAlertBox>

Kittens, seniors, and cats with chronic conditions can dehydrate quickly and should be assessed earlier.

## Could Kidney Disease Be Related?

Chronic kidney disease more often presents with thirst changes, weight loss, and vomiting than diarrhea, but CKD cats can still have GI flare-ups. If your cat has kidney risk factors, review `disease-ckd` and ask your veterinarian whether blood and urine testing is due.

## What To Do at Home First

- Offer fresh water and monitor intake.
- Feed bland, vet-approved diet guidance if instructed.
- Keep a stool log (frequency, consistency, blood/mucus).
- Bring a fresh stool sample to your appointment.

Do not give anti-diarrheal human medications unless prescribed by your veterinarian.
