Drooling (ptyalism) is a symptom, not a diagnosis. In cats, the context is everything: a few drops while kneading on your lap can be harmless, while sudden ropey saliva with pawing at the mouth can be urgent.

## When Drooling Can Be Normal

Mild drooling may be normal if it is:

- brief
- linked to relaxed purring or kneading
- consistent with your cat's long-term pattern
- not paired with pain, appetite changes, or bad breath

## Common Medical Causes

| Cause | What you might notice |
|------|---|
| Dental or gum disease | Bad breath, food dropping, chewing on one side |
| Mouth injury or foreign material | Pawing at mouth, sudden distress |
| Nausea | Lip licking, swallowing repeatedly, reduced appetite |
| Toxins or irritants | Sudden heavy drool, vomiting, tremors |
| Upper respiratory or throat problems | Noisy breathing, trouble swallowing |

## Normal vs Emergency

<HealthAlertBox title="Emergency warning signs">
Go to emergency care now if drooling appears with breathing trouble, repeated vomiting, neurologic signs, severe lethargy, or known toxin exposure. Do not wait to see if this improves overnight.
</HealthAlertBox>

Schedule a same-day or next-day exam if drooling persists more than a day, recurs often, or appears with appetite loss, oral odor, or behavior change.

## What To Do Before the Vet Visit

- Remove possible toxins or houseplants from reach.
- Do not force food into a cat that is nauseated or painful.
- Avoid opening the mouth if your cat is distressed.
- Record videos of episodes to help your veterinarian identify patterns.

Early oral exams matter because dental disease and oral pain are common and very treatable when addressed promptly.
