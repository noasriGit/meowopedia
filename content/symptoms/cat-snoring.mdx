Yes, cats can snore. A soft snore during deep sleep can be normal, especially if your cat sleeps curled with the neck tucked. The concern is change: new, louder, or progressive snoring may point to airway or nasal disease.

## Can Cats Snore, and Is It Normal?

Snoring may be normal when it is:

- occasional
- quiet
- only during deep sleep
- not associated with daytime breathing signs

Snoring is less likely to be normal when it appears suddenly, gets louder over weeks, or occurs with noisy breathing while awake.

## Common Reasons Cats Snore

| Cause | Typical clues |
|------|---|
| Sleep position | Snoring changes when your cat shifts posture |
| Weight gain | Heavier body condition, louder breathing sounds |
| Nasal inflammation or congestion | Sneezing, discharge, noisy inhalation |
| Upper airway narrowing | Persistent noise, possible exercise intolerance |
| Dental or facial disease | Oral odor, facial asymmetry, appetite changes |

## Normal vs Emergency

<HealthAlertBox title="Breathing emergencies">
If your cat shows open-mouth breathing, blue/gray gums, marked abdominal breathing effort, or collapse, seek emergency care immediately.
</HealthAlertBox>

Book a routine exam for any snoring that is new, persistent, or worsening, even if your cat still seems comfortable.

## What Your Vet May Check

- Full oral and nasal exam
- Body weight and body condition
- Upper airway sounds
- Imaging or referral if obstruction is suspected

Early evaluation helps catch treatable issues before breathing becomes more difficult.
