Cats can cough, but it is easy to confuse coughing with hairball retching. True cough is often a lower-airway sign and should not be ignored if it repeats.

## Cough vs Hairball: Quick Difference

| Feature | Coughing | Hairball retching |
|------|---|---|
| Sound | Dry, hacking, sometimes wheezy | Gagging/heaving sound |
| Output | Often none | Often fluid or hair produced |
| Pattern | Repeated episodes over time | Intermittent, usually tied to grooming/hair |

## Common Causes of Cough in Cats

- Feline asthma or chronic bronchitis
- Airway irritation (smoke, dust, aerosols)
- Respiratory infection
- Parasites in some regions
- Less commonly, cardiac or lung structural disease

## Normal vs Emergency

Occasional isolated throat-clearing may occur, but repeated coughing is not considered normal.

<HealthAlertBox title="Emergency breathing signs">
If your cat is open-mouth breathing, has pronounced abdominal breathing effort, appears blue/gray at the gums, or collapses, go to emergency care immediately.
</HealthAlertBox>

Schedule a prompt exam if cough episodes persist more than a few days or increase in frequency.

## What Your Vet May Recommend

- Chest radiographs
- Airway-focused exam
- Trial treatment for asthma/inflammation
- Additional testing when infection or heart disease is suspected

Early treatment can significantly improve comfort and reduce severe flare-ups in cats with chronic airway disease.
