Cats are experts at hiding illness. By the time signs are obvious, disease may already be advanced. That is why subtle behavior and appetite changes matter.

## Common Signs a Cat May Be Sick

- eating less or skipping meals
- hiding or reduced social behavior
- vomiting or diarrhea
- changes in litter box habits
- coughing, sneezing, or noisy breathing
- reduced grooming or unkempt coat
- unexplained weight loss

## Normal Variation vs Warning Signs

Some day-to-day variation is normal, such as a brief appetite dip during stress. Concern rises when changes persist more than 24 hours or cluster together.

| Pattern | Usually lower concern | Higher concern |
|------|---|---|
| Appetite | Slight temporary decrease | Refusing food for a day or more |
| Energy | Briefly less active | Sustained lethargy, hiding, weakness |
| GI signs | One mild episode | Repeated vomiting/diarrhea, blood |
| Urination | Minor variation | Straining, no urine, large sustained change |

## Emergency Signs: Do Not Wait

<HealthAlertBox title="Get emergency veterinary care now">
Open-mouth breathing, collapse, repeated seizures, inability to urinate, severe trauma, continuous vomiting, or blue/gray gums are emergencies.
</HealthAlertBox>

## Chronic Disease Clues (Including CKD)

In middle-aged and senior cats, gradual thirst increase, larger urine clumps, vomiting, and weight loss can indicate chronic disease such as kidney dysfunction. See `disease-ckd` for a focused guide on CKD signs and management.

## What Owners Should Do

- Track changes in appetite, litter box, and behavior.
- Weigh your cat regularly on the same scale.
- Keep preventive visits current, especially for senior cats.
- Contact your veterinarian early when patterns change.

Early action usually means more treatment options and better outcomes.
