Cats absolutely can fart. Most owners just notice it less often than in dogs because cats are smaller and often pass gas quietly. The key issue is frequency and context.

## What Is Normal Gas in Cats?

Mild, occasional flatulence can happen after:

- swallowing extra air while eating quickly
- a minor diet switch
- rich treats or dairy

If your cat is otherwise normal, occasional gas alone is not usually concerning.

## When Farting Suggests a Problem

| Pattern | Why it matters |
|------|---|
| Frequent foul odor | Possible food intolerance or gut imbalance |
| Gas plus diarrhea | GI inflammation, parasites, dietary issue |
| Gas plus vomiting | Broader digestive disease may be present |
| Gas plus weight loss | Malabsorption or chronic disease concern |
| Painful abdomen or straining | Needs prompt veterinary exam |

## Normal vs Emergency

Most gas cases are not emergencies. Treat it as urgent if your cat has:

- repeated vomiting
- bloody stool
- marked abdominal pain
- severe lethargy or collapse

<VetTip title="Do not use human gas medications without veterinary guidance">
Some over-the-counter products are unsafe or unhelpful in cats. Always ask your veterinarian before using home treatments.
</VetTip>

## Practical Next Steps

- Feed measured meals, not large single portions.
- Transition foods slowly over 7-10 days.
- Avoid milk and high-fat table scraps.
- Ask your vet about a fecal test if signs persist.

Persistent flatulence is usually manageable once the underlying digestive trigger is identified.
