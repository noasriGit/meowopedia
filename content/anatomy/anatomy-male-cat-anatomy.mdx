## Short Answer

Yes. Intact male cats normally have testicles (informally called "balls"), with two testes expected in the scrotum after normal development.

## Normal Male Cat Reproductive Anatomy

In sexually mature intact males:

- Two testes are usually palpable in the scrotum
- Testosterone drives secondary sex traits
- Penile spines are testosterone-dependent and visible in intact mature males

After neutering, testicles are surgically removed and hormone levels decline.

## Why Owners Sometimes Do Not See Them

Common reasons:

| Situation | What it means |
|---|---|
| Early-age kitten | Testes may not yet be obvious to owners |
| Previously neutered cat | Testes removed surgically |
| Cryptorchid cat | One or both testes retained in abdomen/inguinal area |

## What Is Cryptorchidism?

Cryptorchidism means one or both testes fail to descend as expected. A retained testicle may still produce hormones, and retained tissue carries higher risks over time, including torsion and some tumors.

Veterinary treatment is surgical removal of retained testicular tissue.

## Signs That Warrant a Vet Exam

- Suspected male with no palpable scrotal testicle
- Ongoing urine spraying or mating behavior after "neuter"
- Penile spine persistence in a presumed neutered adult
- Groin or abdominal discomfort/swelling

Your veterinarian may use palpation, imaging, and hormone-based diagnostics to confirm status.

## Owner Takeaway

Using direct terms is fine: intact male cats normally have testicles; neutered cats do not. If anatomy is unclear or behavior suggests persistent testosterone, ask your veterinarian to rule out retained testicular tissue.
