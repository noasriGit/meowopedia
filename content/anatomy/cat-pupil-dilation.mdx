Cat pupils are dynamic structures that change size quickly to balance vision and protection from light. Wide pupils are often normal in low light or high excitement, but the pattern over time is what matters.

## Normal Reasons Pupils Dilate

| Situation | Why pupils enlarge |
|------|---|
| Dim room or nighttime | Maximize light entry for vision |
| Play/hunting mode | Sympathetic arousal response |
| Startle or fear | "Fight or flight" activation |

These changes should be temporary and responsive to changing light.

## When "Always Dilated" Is Concerning

Persistent or asymmetrical dilation can be associated with:

- eye pain or inflammation
- retinal or optic nerve disease
- systemic hypertension
- neurologic dysfunction
- toxin exposure or medication effects

## Normal vs Emergency

<HealthAlertBox title="Emergency eye and neurologic signs">
Seek urgent care for sudden vision loss, one pupil very different from the other, fixed wide pupils that do not react to light, head trauma, seizures, or collapse.
</HealthAlertBox>

For non-emergency cases, schedule an eye-focused exam if your cat's pupils stay enlarged unusually often, especially with behavior or vision changes.

## What Your Veterinarian May Check

- Pupillary light reflexes
- Blood pressure
- Retinal examination
- Neurologic status

Fast evaluation protects vision and helps identify underlying disease early.
