## What Whiskers Actually Are

Your cat's whiskers are not decorative fur. They are **vibrissae**: highly specialized sensory hairs connected to deep follicles and rich nerve networks.

## Why They Are So Long

Long whiskers improve a cat's ability to gather information before contact. They help detect:

- Airflow changes near obstacles
- Object position close to the face
- Tight-space boundaries
- Prey movement at very short range

Cats are excellent distance and low-light visual hunters, but vision close to the nose is less precise. Whiskers fill that near-field gap.

## Where Whiskers Are Located

Most owners notice the cheek whiskers, but cats also have vibrissae:

- Above the eyes
- On the chin
- On the forelimb/wrist region

These placements support navigation, prey handling, and protective reflexes.

## Do Long Whiskers Mean Better Balance?

Whiskers support spatial awareness, but they are not a simple "balance organ." Balance also depends on the vestibular system (inner ear), vision, musculoskeletal function, and proprioception.

## Should You Trim Them?

No. Trimming whiskers can reduce environmental feedback and may cause disorientation or stress behaviors. Even if whiskers regrow, avoid intentional clipping.

## Bottom Line

Cat whiskers are long because they are precision sensory tools. Their size, placement, and nerve-rich roots help cats map nearby space quickly and safely, especially in dim light or cluttered environments.
