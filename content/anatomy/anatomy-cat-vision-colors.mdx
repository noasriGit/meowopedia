## Short Answer

Cats do not choose colors the way humans do, but their visual system tends to detect **blue-violet and yellow** contrasts better than red-green combinations.

## How Cat Color Vision Works

Cats are generally described as **dichromats**, meaning they rely on two main cone channels rather than three (like humans). This limits color discrimination but still allows meaningful color perception.

Practical result:

- Better contrast in blue/yellow ranges
- Weaker separation of red vs. green tones
- Greater reliance on brightness, movement, and pattern

## What This Means for Toys and Enrichment

If you want visual clarity for toy selection:

| Usually easier to detect | Often less distinct |
|---|---|
| Blue toys on neutral floors | Red toys on brown or green backgrounds |
| Yellow accents in low clutter spaces | Low-contrast pastel-on-pastel designs |

That said, cats prioritize movement and interaction cues over static color.

## Do Cats "Prefer" Colors Emotionally?

There is no strong evidence that cats have human-like emotional preferences for one color family. Owners often interpret color preference when the cat is actually responding to:

- Prey-like movement
- Crinkle/sound profile
- Texture under paws or mouth
- Scent and novelty

## Better Question Than "What Color Do Cats Like?"

A more useful welfare question is:  
**"What visual contrast helps my cat engage safely and confidently?"**

In many homes, high-contrast blue/yellow toy options plus motion-based play produce the best response.

## Bottom Line

Cats do see color, but in a narrower way than humans. For practical enrichment, prioritize blue/yellow contrast, movement, and interactive play design over color alone.
