## Overview

The Persian cat is among the most iconic breeds in feline history. With its dense long coat, broad round head, and large expressive eyes, the Persian has defined the aesthetic standard for luxury companion cats for over a century.

<VetTip title="Flat-Face Considerations">
Persians with extreme brachycephalic features may experience breathing difficulty, tear staining, and dental crowding. When selecting a Persian, discuss facial structure with a reputable breeder or rescue organization.
</VetTip>

## History

Persians were first documented in Europe in the 1600s after traders brought long-haired cats from Persia (modern-day Iran) and Turkey. Victorian England embraced the breed, and selective breeding through the 20th century produced the modern flat-faced show type alongside traditional doll-face lines.

## Temperament & Personality

Persians are typically calm, affectionate, and prefer serene environments. They enjoy lap time and gentle play but are not usually high-energy climbers. Many Persians coexist peacefully with children and other pets when introductions are gradual.

## Health Issues

Persian cats carry breed-specific health risks that owners should monitor proactively:

- **Polycystic kidney disease (PKD)** — genetic condition affecting kidney function
- **Brachycephalic airway syndrome** — breathing difficulty in flat-faced lines
- **Progressive retinal atrophy** — inherited eye disease in some lines
- **Dental malocclusion** — crowding related to skull shape

<HealthAlertBox title="Kidney Health">
Persians have elevated risk for kidney disease. Annual bloodwork and urinalysis after age 7 is strongly recommended.
</HealthAlertBox>

## Grooming

Daily combing with a wide-tooth comb and slicker brush prevents mats in the dense undercoat. Pay special attention to the armpits, belly, and behind the ears. Regular eye cleaning reduces tear staining in flat-faced individuals.

## Nutrition

Persians benefit from diets formulated for long-haired breeds — higher fiber supports hairball control, and kibble shape can accommodate flat facial structure. Always ensure adequate hydration.

## Are Persian Cats Hypoallergenic?

No. Persian cats are not hypoallergenic. Their long dense coat can trap and spread allergens through dander and saliva residue, and no cat breed is truly allergy-free. Some people tolerate individual Persians better than others, but allergy-sensitive households should test exposure before adopting.

## Exercise & Enrichment

Low-intensity play sessions with wand toys and puzzle feeders provide sufficient exercise. Vertical space is less critical than for active breeds, but scratching posts and window perches enrich indoor life.
