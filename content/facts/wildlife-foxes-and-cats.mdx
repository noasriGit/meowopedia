## Short Answer

Foxes **can** kill cats, but healthy adult cats are not a routine prey item in most places.

Red foxes are opportunistic carnivores that usually choose smaller, lower-risk prey such as rodents, rabbits, birds, and insects.

## What the Risk Looks Like in Real Life

Most cat-fox encounters end in avoidance, not predation. Risk rises when a cat is:

- Very small (kitten size)
- Injured, ill, or mobility-limited
- Roaming alone at night in high fox-density areas
- Eating from outdoor food stations that attract wildlife

## Why Adults Are Less Common Targets

Adult cats can scratch, bite, and defend territory effectively. For a fox, that is a higher-risk interaction than hunting mice or rabbits. Predators generally conserve energy and avoid unnecessary injury.

## Key Safety Steps for Owners

Use layered prevention:

1. Keep cats indoors from dusk to dawn.
2. Build or use an enclosed **catio** for outdoor enrichment.
3. Do not leave pet food outside overnight.
4. Secure garbage and compost to reduce fox attractants.
5. Supervise small or medically vulnerable cats outdoors.

## Foxes vs. Other Predators

In many North American neighborhoods, **coyotes** are a more documented risk to cats than foxes. That means outdoor safety planning should account for all local predators, not foxes alone.

## Bottom Line

The phrase "do foxes kill cats" is not a myth, but the risk is often overstated for healthy adult indoor-outdoor cats. The highest-risk group is kittens and vulnerable cats left unsupervised at peak fox activity times.
