## Short Answer

Cats cannot have **Down syndrome** in the human medical sense.

In people, Down syndrome is usually caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21 (trisomy 21). Domestic cats have a different karyotype (38 total chromosomes, arranged as 19 pairs), so they do not have a human chromosome 21 to duplicate.

## Why People Ask This

Some cats have a recognizable set of traits that can look "Down syndrome-like" to owners, such as:

- Flat or broad facial features
- Widely spaced eyes
- Lower muscle tone
- Poor coordination
- Developmental delays

Those signs are real, but the label is usually not accurate. In veterinary medicine, the goal is to identify the specific underlying condition rather than apply a human diagnosis.

## What Might Actually Be Going On

Cats with unusual appearance or neurologic signs may have:

| Possible cause | What owners may notice |
|---|---|
| Congenital craniofacial variation | Different skull/face shape from kittenhood |
| Cerebellar hypoplasia | Wobbly gait, tremors, poor balance |
| Early-life injury or illness | Delayed development or coordination issues |
| Other genetic abnormalities | Atypical growth or behavior patterns |

Some cats live full, happy lives with these differences, especially when their home setup is adapted for safety.

## When to See a Veterinarian

Book an exam promptly if your cat has:

- Trouble eating or drinking
- Frequent falls or head tilt
- Disorientation or episodes of confusion
- Failure to thrive as a kitten
- Sudden change in coordination (especially in adults)

Veterinary workups may include a full neurologic exam, bloodwork, and imaging based on signs.

## Bottom Line

Cats do not get Down syndrome. They can, however, have congenital or neurologic conditions that look superficially similar. A practical, evidence-based approach is to focus on diagnosis, comfort, and long-term support for the individual cat.
