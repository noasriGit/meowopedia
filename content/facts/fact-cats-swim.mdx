## Short Answer

Yes. Most cats can swim.  
No. Most cats do not **prefer** being put in water.

Those two statements are both true.

## Ability vs. Preference

Swimming ability is a basic motor response in many mammals, including domestic cats. But behaviorally, many cats avoid deep water because:

- Their first exposure was forced or stressful
- Wet fur changes body feel and temperature comfort
- They prefer control over distance and contact with water

Cats often tolerate water better when they can choose proximity (for example, batting at faucet water) rather than being submerged.

## Why the Myth Persists

People often confuse "doesn't like bathing" with "cannot swim." A reluctant cat in a bath may panic, which looks like inability but is usually fear and stress.

## Are Some Cats More Water-Friendly?

Yes. Individual variation is large. Some cats:

- Enjoy paw play in sinks and tubs
- Tolerate boat life and shallow water exploration
- Show confidence around moving water

Wild felids such as fishing cats demonstrate that "cats and water never mix" is biologically inaccurate.

## Safety Rules

If your cat is near water:

1. Never force immersion.
2. Keep exits visible and easy.
3. Use supervision at all times.
4. Avoid fast current, cold water, and steep-sided containers.
5. Dry and warm the cat after any accidental soaking.

## Bottom Line

Cats can swim, but most do not enjoy being made to swim. Respecting choice and reducing stress is the key to safe water exposure.
