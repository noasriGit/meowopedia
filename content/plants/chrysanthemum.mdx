## Quick Answer

Yes, chrysanthemums (mums) are toxic to cats. Most exposures cause gastrointestinal and oral irritation, but some cats can also show skin irritation or mild neurologic signs.

Because mums are so common in seasonal bouquets and porch decor, accidental exposure is frequent in fall.

## Why Chrysanthemums Affect Cats

Chrysanthemums contain natural compounds including pyrethrins and related irritants. In cats, these can trigger:

- Drooling
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Reduced appetite
- Occasional wobbliness or depression

Not every case becomes severe, but repeated vomiting can quickly lead to dehydration.

## Common Exposure Situations

Owners often report one of these scenarios:

- Cat chewing petals from a centerpiece
- Outdoor cat nibbling potted mums
- Indoor cat licking leaves after plant trimming
- Cat drinking vase water from mum arrangements

In mixed bouquets, plant labels may not list every stem, so identification can be delayed.

## Symptoms and Timeline

Signs may begin within hours:

- Lip smacking
- Excess salivation
- Vomiting
- Loose stool
- Mild lethargy

Concerning escalation signs:

- Repeated vomiting
- Tremors
- Marked weakness
- Dehydration

Any neurologic change should be treated as urgent.

## What to Do If Your Cat Ate Chrysanthemum

1. Remove access to the plant and all dropped petals
2. Wipe residue from fur and paws
3. Offer fresh water
4. Contact your veterinarian or poison helpline
5. Monitor closely for 24 hours

Do not induce vomiting at home unless instructed by a veterinarian.

## Veterinary Treatment

Treatment depends on severity and time since ingestion:

- GI decontamination in select cases
- Anti-nausea medication
- Fluid therapy
- Dermal decontamination if skin contact occurred
- Monitoring for neurologic signs

Most cats recover well with prompt supportive care.

## Mums vs. Other Plant Risks

Chrysanthemum toxicity is usually moderate compared to emergency-level true lily ingestion.

- **Mums:** GI and irritation signs, occasional neurologic effects
- **True lilies:** potential kidney failure in cats

For lily emergencies, visit [Lily Toxicity in Cats](/plants/lily) (`plant-lily`).

## Home and Garden Prevention

Practical strategies:

- Keep mums out of cat-access rooms
- Avoid mixed bouquet placement on low surfaces
- Clean dropped petals daily
- Label outdoor toxic plants for family awareness

For cat households, seasonal decor should be screened the same way as permanent houseplants.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are chrysanthemums toxic to cats if only smelled?

Smelling alone is usually lower risk than chewing, but direct plant contact and ingestion are the main concerns.

### Are all mum varieties dangerous?

Most common chrysanthemum varieties should be treated as toxic to cats.

### Can one petal make a cat sick?

A tiny amount may cause minimal signs, but sensitive cats can react quickly. Monitor for drooling and vomiting.

### Is chrysanthemum toxicity fatal?

Fatal outcomes are uncommon, but severe dehydration or complications can occur if treatment is delayed.

### What if my cat seems normal after chewing a mum?

Continue monitoring for delayed GI signs and contact your veterinarian for individualized guidance.

## Bottom Line

Chrysanthemums are toxic to cats and should not be left where cats can chew them. Most cases involve drooling, vomiting, and diarrhea, but severe signs can occur in sensitive cats. Fast cleanup and early veterinary consultation significantly improve comfort and recovery.
