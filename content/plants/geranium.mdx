## Quick Answer

Yes, geraniums are poisonous to cats. Most cases cause gastrointestinal upset and lethargy, and some cats develop skin irritation after contact with plant sap or oils.

Because geraniums are common patio and balcony plants, exposure often happens in warm weather.

## Why Geraniums Cause Toxicity

Geranium species contain aromatic compounds, including geraniol and linalool, which can irritate cats when ingested or contacted on skin.

Possible effects:

- Drooling
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Reduced appetite
- Mild depression or weakness
- Skin redness or itchiness

Concentrated oil products are typically higher risk than accidental leaf nibbling.

## Exposure Scenarios

Common real-world incidents include:

- Cat chewing balcony geranium leaves
- Cat rubbing face against scented geranium
- Ingestion of fallen petals
- Contact with geranium-based essential oil products

If oils are involved, urgency increases because concentration is much higher than in the plant.

<WarningBox title="Plant and Oil Risks Are Different">
A bite of leaf and a lick of essential oil are not equivalent exposures. Oil contact can be much more significant and requires rapid veterinary consultation.
</WarningBox>

## Symptoms to Monitor

Most signs begin within hours:

- Nausea and drooling
- Vomiting
- Loose stool
- Low energy

Dermal signs can include:

- Local redness
- Itching
- Pawing or overgrooming at contact sites

Urgent care is needed for persistent vomiting, marked weakness, tremors, or inability to maintain hydration.

## Immediate Steps After Exposure

1. Remove access to the plant
2. Wipe plant residue from fur and paws
3. If dermal contact occurred, rinse with mild soap and water
4. Prevent grooming until cleaned
5. Call your veterinarian or poison helpline with details

Do not induce vomiting at home unless advised by your veterinary team.

## Veterinary Treatment

Care is mainly supportive:

- Anti-nausea medication
- Fluid support
- GI protectants
- Skin decontamination and irritation care
- Observation for neurologic or worsening signs

Most cats improve with early intervention.

## Geraniums in Cat Homes

Risk can be reduced with environmental planning:

- Keep geranium pots out of all cat-access zones
- Avoid indoor overwintering in shared spaces
- Remove fallen petals daily
- Skip geranium oil diffusers entirely

For known plant chewers, replacement with cat-safe alternatives is often the most reliable strategy.

## Geranium vs. True Lily

Geraniums are poisonous, but their toxicity pattern differs from true lily poisoning:

- **Geranium:** GI and skin irritation profile
- **True lily:** severe kidney toxicity risk in cats

For true lily emergency information, see [Lily Toxicity in Cats](/plants/lily) (`plant-lily`).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are all geraniums poisonous to cats?

Common geranium and Pelargonium varieties should be treated as poisonous to cats.

### Are geranium petals less dangerous than leaves?

Both petals and leaves can contribute to toxicity and should not be considered safe.

### Can cats recover from geranium poisoning at home?

Some mild cases do, but persistent GI signs, lethargy, or any neurologic symptom needs veterinary care.

### Is scented geranium tea safe for cats?

No. Prepared products and extracts can increase exposure to active compounds.

### Can touching geranium alone make a cat sick?

Some cats may develop mild skin irritation with contact, especially if they groom afterward.

## Bottom Line

Geraniums are poisonous to cats and can trigger vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy, and skin irritation. Keep both plants and geranium oil products away from cats, clean contact exposures promptly, and seek veterinary advice early if symptoms develop.
