## Toxicity Level

True lilies rank among the **most dangerous plants for cats**. Even minimal exposure — a few pollen grains licked from fur — can trigger acute kidney failure.

<HealthAlertBox title="Life-Threatening">
Treat every lily exposure as an emergency. Bring your cat to a veterinarian immediately, even without visible symptoms.
</HealthAlertBox>

## Symptoms

- Vomiting (often within hours)
- Lethargy and depression
- Loss of appetite
- Increased or decreased urination
- Dehydration
- Acute kidney failure (24–72 hours)

## Emergency Steps

1. **Remove the cat from the plant** and isolate remaining plant material
2. **Do not wait for symptoms** — seek emergency veterinary care
3. **Bring plant identification** or photos if possible
4. **Expect 48+ hours of IV fluid therapy** and kidney monitoring

## Vet Treatment

Treatment is supportive and time-sensitive:

- Induced emesis (if recent ingestion)
- Activated charcoal
- Aggressive IV fluid diuresis
- Serial kidney values (creatinine, SDMA)
- Anti-nausea medications

## Safe Alternatives

Cat-safe greenery includes:

- Cat grass (Avena sativa, Triticum aestivum)
- Catnip (Nepeta cataria)
- Spider plant (non-toxic, mild hallucinogenic effect in some cats)

<WarningBox title="Holiday Risk">
Easter lilies are a leading cause of feline poisoning each spring. Avoid bringing true lilies into homes with cats.
</WarningBox>

## Related Plants

Do not confuse true lilies with similarly named plants. Peace lilies and calla lilies cause oral irritation but are not true Lilium species — still keep them away from cats.
