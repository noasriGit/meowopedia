## Quick Answer

Yes, calla lilies are poisonous to cats. They can cause intense mouth irritation, drooling, and vomiting due to insoluble calcium oxalate crystals.

The key point: calla lilies are **not true lilies**, so their toxicity differs from the emergency kidney-failure pattern seen with Lilium and Hemerocallis species.

## Calla Lily vs. True Lily: Critical Difference

This distinction matters for triage and owner anxiety:

- **Calla lily (Zantedeschia):** oral pain, drooling, GI irritation
- **True lily (Lilium/Hemerocallis):** high-risk acute kidney injury in cats

If you need guidance for true lilies, use [Lily Toxicity in Cats](/plants/lily) (`plant-lily`).

<HealthAlertBox title="Both Are Unsafe, But for Different Reasons">
Do not treat calla lily exposure as harmless. It is usually less likely to cause kidney failure than true lilies, but can still cause severe oral discomfort and dehydration from persistent vomiting.
</HealthAlertBox>

## Why Calla Lilies Cause Symptoms

Calla lilies contain insoluble calcium oxalate crystals that lodge in the tissues of the mouth, tongue, and throat after chewing.

Common effects:

- Burning oral pain
- Heavy drooling
- Pawing at face
- Refusal to eat
- Vomiting

In more severe cases, swelling can make swallowing difficult.

## Symptoms to Watch for

Early signs can start quickly:

- Lip smacking and drooling
- Vocalizing near food
- Mouth pawing
- Vomiting

Escalation signs that need urgent care:

- Repeated vomiting
- Marked lethargy
- Trouble swallowing
- Breathing noise
- Refusal to drink

Cats with pre-existing illness can dehydrate quickly after repetitive GI signs.

## What to Do If Your Cat Ate Calla Lily

1. Remove all plant fragments from access
2. Wipe the mouth and paws with damp gauze
3. Offer small amounts of water
4. Keep your cat calm and monitored indoors
5. Contact your veterinarian or poison helpline

Do not induce vomiting at home unless a veterinarian specifically instructs it.

## Veterinary Care and Recovery

Most cases are treated supportively:

- Oral exam
- Pain control
- Anti-nausea therapy
- Fluids for hydration
- Monitoring until appetite returns

Prognosis is usually good with prompt care, but delayed treatment can prolong discomfort and increase dehydration risk.

## Indoor Decor and Bouquet Risks

Calla lilies are common in floral arrangements and event decor, where cats may gain access unexpectedly.

Risk reduction checklist:

- Avoid calla lilies in cat homes
- Inspect mixed bouquets for hidden lilies
- Keep floral foam and vase water inaccessible
- Educate household members on plant IDs

Plant confusion is common when arrangements use generic labels like "lily."

## Common Misconceptions

### "It says lily, so it must always cause kidney failure."

Not always. The word "lily" appears in many common names. Toxicity depends on botanical group, not label wording.

### "My cat drooled but then seemed better, so we can ignore it."

Temporary improvement can happen, but persistent oral pain or delayed vomiting can follow. Monitoring and veterinary advice are still important.

### "Dried calla lily petals are safer."

Dried tissue can still irritate the mouth and should remain inaccessible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are calla lilies poisonous to cats?

Yes. They are considered poisonous due to oral and gastrointestinal irritation potential.

### Are peace lilies and calla lilies the same?

They are different plants, but both can cause oral irritation in cats and should be kept out of reach.

### Can calla lily poisoning be fatal?

Fatal outcomes are less common than with true lilies, but severe complications can occur if symptoms are ignored.

### Should I go to emergency vet immediately?

If there is heavy drooling, repeated vomiting, breathing change, or marked lethargy, yes. Otherwise call your vet promptly for triage instructions.

### How long do symptoms last?

Mild cases may resolve within 12-24 hours; moderate cases can last longer and may require medication and fluid support.

## Bottom Line

Calla lilies are poisonous to cats and should not be treated as safe decorative plants. They are not true lilies, so the typical pattern is painful oral irritation and GI upset rather than classic lily kidney toxicity. Rapid cleanup, close monitoring, and early veterinary guidance provide the best outcomes.
