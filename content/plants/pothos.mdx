## Quick Answer

Yes, pothos are toxic to cats. They are one of the most commonly chewed houseplants in cat homes and can cause immediate mouth pain, drooling, and vomiting.

The toxicity mechanism is mechanical irritation from microscopic needle-like crystals called **insoluble calcium oxalates**.

## Why Pothos Irritates Cats

When a cat bites pothos leaves or stems, oxalate crystals embed in oral tissues. This can feel like tiny glass shards in the mouth and throat, which explains the sudden dramatic reaction many owners see.

Typical effects:

- Oral burning or stinging
- Drooling
- Pawing at the mouth
- Reduced appetite
- Vomiting

While severe systemic poisoning is less common, the pain response can be intense.

<HealthAlertBox title="Painful But Usually Not Kidney-Toxic">
Pothos exposure is often very uncomfortable but usually not in the same life-threatening category as true lily ingestion. For true lily emergencies, review [Lily Toxicity in Cats](/plants/lily) (`plant-lily`).
</HealthAlertBox>

## Symptoms to Watch

Most signs appear quickly, often within minutes to a few hours:

- Excessive drooling
- Lip smacking
- Refusing food
- Retching or vomiting
- Mild facial swelling

Less common, higher-concern signs:

- Persistent vomiting
- Difficulty swallowing
- Breathing noise after severe oral swelling
- Lethargy from dehydration

If breathing changes or repeated vomiting occur, treat this as urgent.

## What to Do Right Away

1. Remove your cat from the plant
2. Gently wipe the mouth and paws with water-damp gauze
3. Offer a small amount of water
4. Save a plant sample or clear photo
5. Call your veterinarian or poison helpline

Do not give milk, oils, or home remedies unless specifically instructed by a veterinary professional.

## Veterinary Care

Many cases are treated with outpatient supportive care, depending on severity:

- Oral rinse and exam
- Pain control
- Anti-nausea medication
- Fluids for hydration support
- Monitoring for persistent symptoms

Hospitalization may be needed if oral pain is severe, fluid intake is poor, or vomiting continues.

## Can Cats Eat Pothos in Tiny Amounts?

Even tiny amounts can trigger mouth irritation because the crystals are concentrated in plant tissues. Some cats recover quickly after a brief drooling episode, but others experience prolonged discomfort.

Risk factors for worse signs include:

- Repeated chewing behavior
- Small body size (kittens)
- Existing kidney or GI disease
- Concurrent ingestion of fertilizer-treated soil

## Pothos in Mixed Plant Collections

Pothos often hangs low and trails into cat-height zones, making it especially easy to reach. Prevention is usually about accessibility:

- Move all trailing vines above jump range
- Prune dangling stems promptly
- Use barriers in plant shelves
- Replace pothos in low areas with cat-safe alternatives

If your cat is a known foliage chewer, placement changes alone are often insufficient.

## Pothos vs. Monstera vs. Lily

These plants are often confused in social media posts and emergency calls:

- **Pothos:** oxalate oral irritation, GI upset
- **Monstera:** similar oxalate mechanism and signs
- **True lily:** kidney failure risk and medical emergency

Accurate plant ID changes urgency decisions. When uncertain, always assume higher risk until a veterinarian confirms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are all pothos varieties toxic?

Most common pothos varieties, including golden pothos and marble queen types, should be considered toxic to cats.

### Can pothos cause long-term damage?

Long-term injury is uncommon in mild cases, but severe untreated dehydration or complications from prolonged vomiting can create secondary problems.

### Is satin pothos safe for cats?

No. Satin pothos is generally treated as unsafe for cats due to similar irritation potential.

### Can my cat recover without a vet visit?

Some mild cases do, but if drooling is heavy, vomiting repeats, or appetite does not return quickly, veterinary care is recommended.

### Does dried pothos remain toxic?

Dried plant material can still irritate oral tissues and should not be considered safe.

## Bottom Line

Pothos are toxic to cats and are a frequent cause of painful oral irritation in indoor homes. Most exposures are not fatal, but they can be severe enough to require medication and hydration support. Remove access immediately, monitor closely, and call your veterinarian if signs persist or worsen.
