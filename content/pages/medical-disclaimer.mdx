**Meowopedia does not provide veterinary medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.** The health, disease, symptom, nutrition, and care content on this site is for general educational purposes only.

Always consult a licensed veterinarian regarding your cat's health. Never disregard professional veterinary advice or delay seeking it because of something you read on Meowopedia.

## Educational purpose only

Articles about diseases, symptoms, medications, parasites, emergencies, anatomy, and related topics are written to help cat owners understand feline health concepts. They are not a substitute for:

- A physical examination by a veterinarian
- Diagnostic testing (blood work, imaging, cultures, etc.)
- Prescription medications or treatment plans
- Emergency intervention

Your veterinarian has access to your cat's full medical history, can perform hands-on assessment, and can make individualized recommendations. Meowopedia cannot.

## When to contact your veterinarian

Contact your veterinarian promptly if your cat shows any of the following:

- Difficulty breathing, open-mouth breathing, or blue/pale gums
- Collapse, seizures, or inability to stand
- Repeated vomiting or diarrhea, especially with blood
- Straining to urinate or producing no urine (this can be life-threatening in male cats)
- Ingestion of a known or suspected toxin
- Trauma (falls, bites, car accidents)
- Loss of appetite for more than 24 hours
- Significant lethargy or behavior change
- Eye injury or sudden blindness
- Swollen abdomen with pain or distress

When in doubt, call your veterinarian or an emergency veterinary clinic. It is always better to ask than to wait.

## Poison and toxin emergencies

If you suspect your cat has ingested a toxic substance — including certain foods, plants, medications, or household chemicals — contact a poison control center immediately:

- **ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center**: (888) 426-4435 (fee may apply)
- **Pet Poison Helpline**: (855) 764-7661 (fee may apply)

Have the product label, plant name, or substance description ready. Do not induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by a veterinary professional.

## How our health content is produced

Health-related articles on Meowopedia follow our [editorial standards](/editorial-standards), including review by credentialed veterinarians where appropriate. Despite this review:

- Guidance evolves as veterinary medicine advances
- Individual cats vary in age, breed, health status, and medications
- Articles cannot account for every clinical scenario

We display **lastReviewed** dates on articles so you can assess freshness. If content appears outdated, please report it to [hello@meowopedia.com](mailto:hello@meowopedia.com).
