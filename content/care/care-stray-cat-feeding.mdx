If you care for stray or community cats, the healthiest default is simple:

**feed complete, balanced cat food on a consistent schedule and always provide clean water.**

This improves body condition and is safer than feeding random scraps.

## Best Food Options for Stray Cats

Choose commercial foods labeled complete and balanced for the life stage.

### Dry food

Pros:
- Practical in outdoor feeding stations
- Easy to portion and store

Cons:
- Lower moisture
- Can go stale or attract pests if left out too long

### Wet food

Pros:
- Higher moisture intake
- Often very palatable

Cons:
- Spoils quickly outdoors
- Needs faster pickup and cleanup

Many caregivers use mixed feeding: wet food at set meal times, dry food in controlled amounts.

## What Not to Feed

Avoid common mistakes:

- Cow's milk (many cats are lactose intolerant)
- Heavily seasoned leftovers
- Raw bones or unsafe raw diets in unmanaged settings
- Foods toxic to cats (onion, garlic, chocolate, xylitol-containing products)

These options are either nutritionally incomplete, unsafe, or both.

## Feeding Schedule That Works

Consistent timing helps colony stability and reduces conflict.

Recommended approach:

- Feed once or twice daily at the same times
- Place food in the same clean location
- Remove leftovers after 20 to 30 minutes (faster in heat)
- Wash bowls regularly

Predictable feeding also helps when coordinating trap-neuter-return (TNR), medical checks, or rescue intake.

## Portioning and Body Condition

Not all strays need the same calories. Intake should reflect:

- Body condition (thin, ideal, overweight)
- Weather and energy demand
- Pregnancy/lactation status
- Age (kitten vs adult)

Kittens, pregnant queens, and nursing queens need higher energy intake than sedentary adults.

<VetTip title="Use body condition, not guesswork">
Overfeeding can be as harmful as underfeeding. Even outdoor cats can become overweight when calories are not matched to need.
</VetTip>

## Water and Station Hygiene

Fresh water is non-negotiable. Replace daily; more often in summer. In cold weather, use freeze-resistant bowls when possible.

Keep feeding sites clean to reduce:

- Insect and rodent attraction
- Bacterial growth
- Neighborhood complaints

Cleanliness protects cats and improves long-term community support for your feeding program.

## Medical Red Flags While Feeding

You may be the first person to notice illness. Seek veterinary/rescue support when a cat has:

- Marked weight loss
- Ongoing diarrhea or vomiting
- Drooling, oral pain, or inability to chew
- Lethargy, limping, or respiratory distress

Early intervention can prevent suffering and reduce disease spread in colonies.

## Bottom Line

For stray cats, feed complete cat food, portion consistently, keep water available, and maintain strict station hygiene. This is the safest and most medically sound baseline for daily community-cat care.
