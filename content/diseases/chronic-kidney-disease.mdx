## Symptoms

Early CKD is often subtle. Watch for:

- Polydipsia and polyuria (increased drinking and urination)
- Weight loss and muscle wasting
- Decreased appetite
- Vomiting and nausea
- Poor coat quality
- Bad breath (uremic odor)

## Causes & Risk Factors

CKD results from progressive nephron loss. Contributing factors include:

- Age-related degeneration
- Genetic predisposition (Persian PKD)
- Prior acute kidney injury
- Chronic urinary obstruction
- Dental disease (chronic bacteremia)

## Diagnosis

Diagnosis combines clinical signs with:

- **SDMA and creatinine** blood tests
- **Urinalysis** (USG, proteinuria)
- **Blood pressure** measurement
- **IRIS staging** for treatment planning
- **Ultrasound** to assess kidney architecture

## Treatment

Management is multimodal:

1. **Renal diet** — reduced phosphorus, controlled protein
2. **Hydration** — subcutaneous fluids when indicated
3. **Phosphate binders** — if diet alone insufficient
4. **Anti-nausea medications** — maropitant, mirtazapine
5. **Blood pressure control** — amlodipine when hypertensive

<ExpertNote title="Staging Matters">
Treatment intensity should match IRIS stage. Over-treating early-stage cats or under-treating advanced cases both reduce outcomes.
</ExpertNote>

## Prevention

While not all CKD is preventable:

- Annual senior bloodwork starting at age 7
- Dental care to reduce bacteremia
- Adequate hydration and wet food
- Genetic screening in predisposed breeds

## Emergency Signs

Seek immediate care if your cat:

- Has not eaten for 24+ hours
- Is vomiting repeatedly
- Shows extreme lethargy or collapse
- Has not urinated in 12+ hours

## Recovery & Prognosis

CKD is managed, not cured. With consistent treatment, many cats maintain good quality of life. Regular rechecks every 3–6 months allow treatment adjustment as the disease progresses.
