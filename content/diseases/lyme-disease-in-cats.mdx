Yes, cats can get Lyme disease bacteria from infected tick bites. However, compared with dogs, cats appear less likely to develop classic clinical Lyme disease.

## How cats are exposed

The risk pathway is straightforward:

1. Tick attaches to cat
2. Tick remains attached long enough to transmit bacteria
3. Cat may become infected

Outdoor cats in wooded or grassy tick-endemic areas face the highest exposure risk.

## Do infected cats always get sick?

No. Many exposed cats do not develop obvious illness. When signs occur, they can include:

- Fever
- Lethargy
- Reduced appetite
- Stiffness or lameness

These signs overlap with many other feline conditions, so diagnosis requires caution.

## Diagnosis challenges

Veterinarians may use:

- Tick exposure history
- Physical exam
- Blood testing and broader differential workup

A positive exposure test does not always prove Lyme is the cause of symptoms, so clinical context is essential.

<VetTip title="Think broader than one test">
Because Lyme signs are nonspecific in cats, veterinarians often evaluate for other infectious, orthopedic, and inflammatory causes in parallel.
</VetTip>

## Treatment and outlook

When Lyme-associated illness is suspected, treatment may include:

- Targeted antibiotics
- Pain and inflammation support
- Monitoring for response and alternate diagnoses

Most cats with mild signs improve with timely care, but persistent lameness or systemic illness needs follow-up.

## Prevention strategy

- Use cat-safe tick prevention recommended by your veterinarian
- Check coat and skin after outdoor exposure
- Keep grass and brush trimmed around home areas
- Remove ticks promptly and correctly

Lyme prevention in cats is mainly about avoiding tick attachment in the first place.
