Yes, cats can get "parvo." In cats, this disease is called **feline panleukopenia** and is caused by feline parvovirus. It is one of the most serious infectious diseases in unvaccinated kittens.

## How cats get feline parvo

The virus spreads through contaminated:

- Feces
- Food/water bowls
- Bedding and cages
- Hands, clothing, and equipment

It is hardy in the environment, so outbreaks can be difficult to control in shelters and multi-cat settings.

## Symptoms of feline panleukopenia

Signs can progress quickly:

- High fever, then low temperature in advanced disease
- Severe lethargy
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea (sometimes bloody)
- Dehydration
- Refusal to eat

Kittens are at highest risk for rapid deterioration.

<WarningBox title="Emergency disease in kittens">
Any unvaccinated kitten with vomiting, diarrhea, and lethargy should be seen immediately. Delay can be life-threatening.
</WarningBox>

## Diagnosis

Veterinarians diagnose based on:

- History and vaccination status
- Physical exam
- CBC showing low white blood cell count
- Fecal or antigen testing where appropriate

No single test should be interpreted without clinical context.

## Treatment

There is no direct cure for the virus itself, so care is supportive and often intensive:

- IV fluids and electrolyte correction
- Anti-nausea therapy
- Nutritional support
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics for secondary bacterial risk
- Isolation nursing care

Early treatment significantly improves outcomes.

## Prevention

Prevention is highly effective:

- Follow core kitten and adult vaccine schedules
- Isolate sick cats promptly
- Use appropriate disinfection protocols
- Avoid exposing unvaccinated kittens to unknown cats

Vaccination is the single most important protection against feline parvovirus.
