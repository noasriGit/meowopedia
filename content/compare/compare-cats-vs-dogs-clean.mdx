## Short Answer

By self-grooming behavior, cats are usually cleaner than dogs.  
By total household hygiene impact, the answer is more nuanced.

## Why Cats Get the "Cleaner" Reputation

Cats are highly efficient self-groomers. They spend substantial daily time licking, cleaning, and maintaining coat condition. Their tongue papillae are adapted to distribute saliva through fur, helping with debris removal and thermoregulation.

Dogs groom themselves too, but most rely more on human-assisted bathing, brushing, and coat maintenance.

## Cleanliness Depends on the Metric

| Metric | Typical winner |
|---|---|
| Self-grooming intensity | Cats |
| Need for owner bathing | Usually dogs |
| Outdoor dirt tracking risk | Depends on lifestyle, often cats |
| Allergen burden for sensitive humans | Variable, neither is universally "safe" |

## Important Caveat: Clean Looking != Low Allergen

A cat can look spotless and still contribute significant environmental allergen load, because allergens are linked to saliva and skin proteins, not visible dirt.

## Where Dogs Can Be Cleaner

In some homes, a well-groomed indoor dog with strict bathing and coat care may produce fewer hygiene complaints than:

- A poorly managed litter box situation
- Multi-cat homes with high shedding and limited brushing
- Cats with obesity or arthritis that reduce self-grooming quality

## Bottom Line

Cats generally win on self-maintenance and routine grooming behavior. But "cleaner pet" depends on owner care, environment, coat type, and whether you are discussing odor, fur, or allergy burden.
