## Short Answer

Yes. Dogs can be allergic to cat dander.  
In practice, this usually appears as part of **canine atopic dermatitis** (environmental allergy disease).

## What "Allergic to Cats" Means in Dogs

Most dogs are not allergic to "cat hair" itself. The trigger is typically proteins in:

- Dander (shed skin cells)
- Dried saliva on fur
- Environmental dust carrying those particles

Cat exposure may be one trigger among many (dust mites, pollens, molds, etc.).

## Common Symptoms in Dogs

Dogs usually show skin and ear signs rather than human-style sneezing:

- Chronic itch (face, paws, ears, belly)
- Recurrent ear infections
- Red, inflamed skin
- Paw licking/chewing
- Secondary yeast or bacterial skin infections

## How Vets Confirm the Problem

Diagnosis is stepwise:

1. Rule out fleas, mites, and infection-only causes.
2. Evaluate diet if food allergy is possible.
3. Assess environmental trigger pattern.
4. Use allergy testing when needed to build treatment plans.

## Management in Multi-Pet Homes

Most homes do not need separation or rehoming. Better outcomes usually come from:

- Consistent medical management (itch control + infection control)
- Cleaning soft surfaces where dander accumulates
- HEPA filtration and regular vacuuming
- Bathing/grooming plans tailored by your veterinarian

## Bottom Line

Dogs can absolutely react to cats allergically, but this is usually manageable. Think "multifactor atopy management," not "single trigger panic."
