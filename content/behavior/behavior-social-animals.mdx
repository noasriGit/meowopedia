Cats are best described as **socially flexible**. They are not pack animals like dogs, but they are also not strictly asocial.

## What feline sociality looks like

In resource-rich environments, related females and familiar cats may form loose colonies. In low-resource settings, cats may space out and interact less.

## Household implications

- Compatibility matters more than species-wide rules.
- Resource abundance lowers social friction.
- Slow introductions improve long-term outcomes.

## Signs your cat is socially comfortable

- Relaxed shared spaces
- Cooperative routines without blocking
- Mutual grooming or nearby rest

## Social behavior with humans

Many cats direct affiliative behaviors toward people, including licking, slow blinking, and chest/feet sleeping. Some also pair social contact with kneading (`behavior-making-biscuits`).
