Zoomies are short, intense bursts of running, jumping, and direction changes. In behavior language, they are often called FRAPs (frenetic random activity periods).

## Why zoomies happen

### Predatory energy release
Indoor cats still carry the hunt sequence. Without enough stalking and chasing outlets, energy can build and explode into sprints.

### Time-of-day biology
Many cats peak at dawn and dusk, which is why evening and early morning zoomies are common.

### Post-litter or post-groom arousal
Some cats sprint right after the litter box or grooming because arousal spikes briefly.

## Normal zoomies checklist

- Episodes are short
- Body stays loose and playful
- Cat settles afterward
- No limping, crying, or fear behaviors

## Preventing disruptive zoomies

Use two to three daily play sessions that mimic hunt patterns: stalk, chase, pounce, then a small meal. Predictable routines reduce nighttime outbursts.

<VetTip title="Different from kneading">
If your cat alternates high-energy zoomies with calm kneading rituals, both can be normal. See `behavior-making-biscuits` for the low-arousal comfort behavior.
</VetTip>
