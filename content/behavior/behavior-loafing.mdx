A cat loaf is the classic "bread" pose: paws tucked under the chest, body compact, tail wrapped or resting close. Most of the time, loafing is efficient, comfortable, and normal.

## The loaf's purpose

Loafing helps cats conserve heat while resting lightly. Compared with fully stretched sleep positions, a loaf allows quicker movement if something changes.

## Read the whole body, not just the shape

| Loaf type | Common meaning |
|---|---|
| Soft eyes, neutral ears, slow blink | Relaxed and content |
| Tight face, whiskers forward, tense shoulders | Aroused or uncertain |
| Hunched, reluctant to move, hiding | Possible discomfort or pain |

## Breed note: Persians and compact postures

Cats with stockier builds and calm temperaments, including many Persians, may appear in loaf position frequently. See `breed-persian` for breed-specific posture and comfort habits.

## When to call the vet

Call your vet if loafing is new and paired with appetite loss, vomiting, vocalizing, or breathing changes. Posture alone is not diagnostic, but sudden pattern shifts matter.

Some cats loaf, then transition into kneading before sleep. That sequence can reflect an affiliative comfort loop; read `behavior-making-biscuits` for details.
</ExpertNote>
