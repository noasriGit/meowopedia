Many cats nibble grass. That surprises people because cats are obligate carnivores, but plant chewing is still common behavior.

## Why cats eat grass

1. Sensory curiosity (texture and movement)
2. Mild digestive stimulation
3. Enrichment for indoor cats
4. Occasional self-induced emesis after overeating or hair accumulation

## Risk map

### Usually low risk
- Clean, untreated lawn grass
- Cat grass grown indoors (wheat/oat/barley)

### Higher risk
- Chemically treated outdoor lawns
- Unknown decorative houseplants
- Large, rapid ingestion followed by repeated vomiting

<WarningBox title="Grass vs. toxic plants">
Grass itself is often fine. The bigger danger is confusion with toxic plants (for example lilies) or chemical residues on leaves.
</WarningBox>

## What owners should do

Offer a dedicated pot of cat grass, rotate safe chew enrichment, and remove access to unsafe plants. If your cat repeatedly vomits after plant eating, schedule a workup for GI disease, parasites, or diet intolerance.
