Cats bury feces primarily as a scent-control behavior. In wild and feral contexts, scent placement helps avoid conflict or communicate social position.

## Why burying is common

- Reduces odor cues in shared territory
- Supports perceived safety around predators or rivals
- Reinforces toileting routine in a preferred substrate

## Why covering patterns change

| Change | Common explanation |
|---|---|
| Partial covering | Litter depth/texture mismatch |
| No covering | Stress, box conflict, or different signaling |
| Excessive scratching | Frustration with dirty box or substrate |

## Improve burying behavior

Keep boxes clean, provide unscented clumping litter, and use large boxes with enough depth for digging. In multi-cat homes, spread boxes across separate zones.
