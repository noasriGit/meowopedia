Yes, female cats spray. It is less common than in intact males, but it happens in both intact and spayed females.

## Spraying vs. inappropriate urination

Spraying is communication behavior. Cats typically stand, back up to a vertical surface, lift and quiver the tail, and release a small amount of urine.

Inappropriate urination is usually a larger puddle on horizontal surfaces and can be linked to litter box aversion, pain, or mobility issues.

## Why female cats spray

- Territorial stress (new cat, outside cats at windows)
- Social conflict in multi-cat homes
- Reproductive hormones in intact females
- Environmental instability (moves, schedule shifts)

## Practical treatment plan

1. Rule out urinary disease first.
2. Clean marked sites with enzymatic cleaner.
3. Reduce visual access to outside cats when needed.
4. Increase resources: one litter box per cat plus one extra.
5. Use behavior enrichment and predictable routines.

<HealthAlertBox title="Medical check first">
Painful urination, frequent trips, blood in urine, or vocalizing in the box are urgent signs. Seek prompt veterinary care.
</HealthAlertBox>
